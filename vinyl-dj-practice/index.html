How to practice
  1. Load a track on Deck A, hit Play.
  2. Load a second track on Deck B. Hold CUE to preview in your headphones (split-cue).
  3. Drag the pitch fader on Deck B until the tempo sounds close.
  4. Tap Nudge + / Nudge − or drag the platter to push the beat into phase.
  5. Release CUE on a downbeat, slide the crossfader, and keep nudging to hold phase.
  6. Switch to Vinyl mode to hide BPM numbers and mix purely by ear.
📚 Lesson 1 — Hear which record is faster
Before you can match, you must reliably hear which deck is ahead. Load two records, play both, and just listen for a minute.
  • If the beats "gallop" (ba-da…ba-da), they're close but not matched.
  • The kick that lands early belongs to the faster record. If B's kick pulls ahead of A's, B is fast — pitch it down.
  • Drill: set B's pitch anywhere, guess "faster or slower" out loud, then hit Reveal BPM to check. Ten reps. This one skill is 80% of beatmatching.
📚 Lesson 2 — Ride the pitch, don't set it
Beginners hunt for the "perfect" fader position. It doesn't exist — real records drift (turn on Worn record to simulate it).
  • Get roughly close with the fader, then stop staring at it.
  • Correct drift with small nudges (platter drag or nudge buttons), and only move the fader when you keep having to nudge the same direction — that means your base tempo is off.
  • Nudge fixes phase (beats landing together right now). Fader fixes tempo (whether they stay together). Learn to feel which problem you have.
📚 Lesson 3 — Cueing like vinyl
On turntables you drop the record on a downbeat by hand. Simulate it:
  • Hold CUE on the incoming deck — it plays from the cue point while held, and snaps back on release. That's your needle-drop rehearsal.
  • Release-and-rehold in rhythm: "…3, 4, drop". When your drops consistently land on A's downbeat, press Play instead of releasing.
  • Set the cue point on the first kick of a phrase (use Set Cue while paused for precision).
📚 Lesson 4 — The long blend (the Hunee/Antal move)
Dekmantel-style sets live on long, patient blends across genres and tempos:
  • Keep both records matched for 2+ minutes before finishing the transition. Set a timer. If phase slips, nudge — don't restart.
  • Swap basslines with EQ: cut the incoming deck's LOW, blend mids/highs first, then trade the bass in one move on a phrase boundary.
  • Try blending Disco (≈114) into Afro (≈118): pitch disco up ~+3.5%, or afro down. Hear how much character pitch changes — that's a real decision vinyl DJs make.
📚 Practice ladder — from "I suck" to confident
  1. Faster/slower drill (Easy mode) — 10 correct guesses in a row.
  2. Trainer, Warm-up — score 80+ three times in a row.
  3. Trainer, Club — score 80+ three times in a row, no phase meter.
  4. Blind blend — Vinyl mode, hold a blend 2 minutes, bass swap, no reveals.
  5. Trainer, Dekmantel — worn records that drift while you match. Score 70+ here and you're genuinely beatmatching.
  6. Cross-genre — disco→afro→house→techno, climbing 114→128, one blend each.
Little and often beats marathon sessions: 15 focused minutes a day.
